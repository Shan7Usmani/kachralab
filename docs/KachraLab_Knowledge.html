1. Executive summary

KachraLab changes municipal sanitation from activity proof to outcome proof. Instead of paying when a truck is dispatched or a photo is uploaded, the platform keeps a persistent memory of each waste hotspot, scores its risk, diagnoses likely causes, verifies 14 consecutive clean days and only then releases the retained 60% of the contract.

The delivered app is a Next.js 16 + TypeScript web system with a real OpenStreetMap view, browser geolocation, a server-side engine API, role-based human workflows, tamper-evident evidence logging and an exportable dossier. It runs on Vercel with no external AI, database or payment credentials.

Core sentence: A ticket ends; a case remembers.

2. The problem

The current loop is: citizen reports a pile, a truck is dispatched, a photo is uploaded, the ticket closes - and the same spot refills in 3 to 5 days. The system rewards a one-time action without proving the site stayed clean. Three gaps follow:

  • No hotspot memory: every complaint is isolated, so recurrence is invisible.
  • Root cause ignored: the visible pile is cleared, but missed collection windows, missing bins or night dumping remain.
  • Activity treated as outcome: dispatch proof and closure photos replace sustained verification.

The pitch deck cites 70% of the sanitation budget being wasted on repeat cleanups. That figure is illustrative.

3. Product thesis

Municipal payments should be linked to a verified outcome: the hotspot must stay clean for 14 days and waste must not simply move into the surrounding 150 m halo. KachraLab makes that outcome measurable, explainable and auditable.

Five capabilities

  1. 30 m spatial memory - Haversine clustering turns repeat reports into one persistent case.
  2. Explainable risk engine - a transparent 0-100 score with a monsoon multiplier.
  3. Root-cause diagnostics - rule-based hypotheses from waste type, timing and proximity.
  4. 14-day verification + 150 m halo - detects recurrence and displacement.
  5. 40/60 outcome-linked SLA - 40% paid on clearance, 60% held until verification passes.

4. Algorithms and rules

4.1 Haversine distance

Used for clustering and spillover. Earth radius r = 6,371,000 m. Distance d between report and hotspot centroid uses the great-circle formula. Decision rule:

  • d ≤ 30 m: merge into the existing case; increment recurrence.
  • 30 m < d ≤ 150 m while a case is under watch: flag spillover displacement.
  • d > 150 m: create a new independent hotspot node.

4.2 Risk formula

Risk = (0.35*Recurrence + 0.25*Hazard + 0.25*Proximity + 0.15*Duration) * M_rain. Factors are normalised 0-100. Recurrence reflects 30-day report frequency; Hazard reflects toxicity/leachate; Proximity reflects drains, hospitals and schools; Duration reflects unresolved days. M_rain is 1.00 dry or 1.35 monsoon. Final display score is capped at 100; raw value is also shown.

4.3 Verification and escrow

A case begins with Day 0 clearance. Fourteen checkpoints must be clean. A failure or spillover flips status to RECURRING and the 60% hold is withheld. Fourteen clean days with no spillover release the hold.

7. Main console

The console opens on GZ-042. The hotspot register is hidden by default and can be opened from the map header. The real Leaflet map pans when a marker is selected; the amber circle is 30 m and the blue dashed halo is 150 m. The right panel shows risk, factor contribution, 14-day timeline, root-cause hypotheses and the 40/60 ledger. The rain toggle applies the 1.35 monsoon multiplier to every score.

The RUN DEMO action returns to the console and auto-plays the narrated-free guided sequence with visual captions and spotlight highlights. Voice narration was removed.

8. Human loop

The human side answers "who reports spillover and how?" Humans and cameras are sensors; the halo is the judge.

  • Citizen: attaches a photo, uses live GPS or a preset pin. The platform classifies the pin as recurrence, spillover or new node.
  • Inspector: logs a Day 7 or Day 14 visit with photo and pin. A clean pin advances the checkpoint; a pin in the halo flags spillover and withholds the hold.
  • Contractor: installs bins, fixes the collection beat or desilts the drain. Each action changes the live risk factors.
  • Commissioner: reviews cases and explicitly releases or withholds the 60% payment.

Live location uses navigator.geolocation with high accuracy. Browser permission is required and HTTPS is used on Vercel. If location is denied, preset pins keep the demo working.

9. Proof engines

The PROOF page is grouped into three accordions:

  • Model: intervention sliders for bins, collection beat and drain cleaning; role lens over the same data.
  • Evidence: Canvas-based photo spoof audit with same-landmark/spoof demo pairs; SHA-256 hash chain with verify button.
  • Business: ROI simulator for contract value, wards and recurrence drop; zero-download WhatsApp/QR intake mock; dossier download.

The dossier is a JSON evidence packet containing the risk model, intervention settings, photo audit and ledger entries. The photo audit compares downsampled luminance between before/after images - it is a rule-based demonstration, not a trained vision model.

10. Five-minute presentation script

  1. 30 sec - cold open: "A pile is reported, a truck comes, a photo is uploaded, the ticket closes - and the spot refills. We don't pay for clean photos; we pay for clean sites."
  2. 90 sec - RUN DEMO: Let the console auto-play. Point at the report merge, monsoon score change, Day 5 red checkpoint, 150 m spillover, withheld ₹7,200 and then the released escrow branch.
  3. 45 sec - map: Explain that this is OpenStreetMap with true 30 m and 150 m radii. Click two markers and show the case file changing.
  4. 90 sec - FIELD: Submit a citizen pin inside the halo, log an inspector visit, run a contractor fix, then let the commissioner decide the hold.
  5. 60 sec - PROOF: Move a slider, run the spoof pair audit, verify the hash chain, download the dossier.
  6. 30 sec - IMPACT: Close with buyers, roadmap and the disclaimer that the cloud stack is future state.
If only three minutes are available, do steps 1, 2 and 4.

Where is the data from?Fictional illustrative data, labelled throughout.

Why 14 days?The refill cycle is 3-5 days; 14 days covers recurrence, displacement and a full collection-beat cycle.

12. Limitations and disclaimers

  • All locations, wards, reports, scores and market figures are illustrative.
  • Edge-Vision is a deterministic rule-based simulation, not a trained model.
  • The map requires internet access for OpenStreetMap tiles.
  • No authentication, database, real payments, WhatsApp/Twilio integration or municipal ERP exists.
  • The hash chain is client-side evidence, not server-signed legal proof.
  • LocalStorage can be cleared or edited; RESET restores seed state.

13. Production roadmap

  1. Phase 01 - Dashcam auto-scouting: edge cameras on municipal trucks for passive daily hotspot detection.
  2. Phase 02 - Smart-contract escrow: 40/60 payout triggered through municipal ERP after 14 clean days.
  3. Phase 03 - Swachh Survekshan API: one-click export of verified 14-day eradication dossiers.

Production architecture from the deck: Gemini 2.5 Flash Vision, PostgreSQL/PostGIS, FastAPI and Node.js, Next.js with Tailwind and Mapbox GL, Twilio WhatsApp with dynamic QR.

15. Glossary

Hotspot: a persistent case created from clustered reports. Halo: the 150 m displacement zone. Checkpoint: one day of the 14-day watch. Escrow: the retained 60% payment. Spillover: waste moved just outside the cluster but inside the halo. Dossier: exported evidence packet.

Final handoff line: The project proves that cleanup can be paid for only when the outcome is verified, not merely photographed.
